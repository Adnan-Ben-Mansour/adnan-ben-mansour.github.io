Dense Per-Step Rewards for Reinforcement-Learned Reading Order in Scene Text Recognition

A. Ben Mansour, A. Karine, D. Naccache

Paper →

Abstract

Recovering the reading order of irregular scene text is naturally a discrete problem: a permutation of image patches. Gradient-based recognizers avoid this discreteness, either with autoregressive attention decoders, which are slow at inference, or with continuous feature-rearrangement modules in front of a CTC head, which keep CTC's speed but blend patches softly, constrain the recoverable layouts, and do not expose the order they recover. We keep the permutation discrete and learn it with reinforcement learning: a lightweight actor scores the patches, a Plackett-Luce policy samples an ordering, and a standard CTC head transcribes the reordered features. The standard approach rewards only the completed permutation, and over an action space of size $N!$ this terminal reward fails, as credit cannot be assigned to individual placements. We observe that the Plackett-Luce policy already factorizes into one action per placement, and derive a matching dense per-step reward from the posterior that CTC's forward-backward pass computes anyway. Together with an annealed entropy bonus and sampling temperature, this trains the full decoder from scratch, in a single stage, with no pre-trained weights. On 13 regular and irregular benchmarks, the resulting model approaches the accuracy of continuous rearrangement on the same encoder and training budget, while remaining lightweight and exposing an explicit, directly inspectable reading order.
